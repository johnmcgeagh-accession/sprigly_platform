Reshape moment — after

Round 1 put the whole diff on screen as a panel above the day. Round 2 collapses it to a fixed-height summary chip — a single 48px bar naming the counts, with an expand control and a dismiss. The chip never grows, so a one-line change and a fourteen-item brief cost the same vertical space, and the day underneath is never pushed off screen by a receipt.

The highlight state on changed posts is independent of the chip: dismissing the summary does not un-mark what changed, and the marks persist until the next visit.

What is recorded, and what is not

  • Frame A’s chip reads “1 added” and no more. The Build D report records the classifier’s output, the ten approved posts and zero structure drift — it does not record the receipt lines that application produced. So the chip counts only the delta the report establishes. A launch arc that balances its removes against its adds would have produced replacements; none is claimed here because none is written down.
  • Frame B is a verbatim receipt from docs/reports/ivy-t-rehearsal-failures.md — three Added and three Replaced lines with their real dates. It is also the case that makes the chip necessary: that receipt is the misrouted series (F1), where the client asked for three posts three weeks apart and got three inside four days at the cost of three pillar posts. A summary that names what it replaced is how a client catches that before approving.
  • The chip’s verbs are the receipt’s verbs — added, moved, replaced. The round-2 brief suggested “changed” for the third; replaced is kept because the difference between “this post was edited” and “this post was removed and another took its slot” is exactly the thing a client needs to see.
  • “New” marks and changedIds. The marker already exists — changedIds on the receipt drives it, held in memory and gone on reload. Round 2 makes it independent of the chip, which is a client-side change only.
