Approval — the one door that spends money, adapted to width

A centred modal, not a sheet. A bottom sheet is a phone shape; 1440px of one would be a wall, and the decision it carries is small, deliberate, and exactly the same size on every screen. 480px is as wide as three counts and two sentences ever need to be. The mobile spec already anticipated this — §10 names the approval sheet as one of the pieces that is built once and placed differently.

It is still two steps, and that is a product constraint rather than a UI preference. The Generate pill opens the consequence; the consequence has its own commit. Approval spends money and writes content the client will be asked to publish, and a single mis-click must not reach it — which is more true with a mouse than with a thumb, not less.

Zero rows are omitted, never printed. A month of ten single posts genuinely has nothing to say about hooks, and “0 hooks” is padding on the one screen whose job is to state a consequence plainly.

The scrim leaves the month legible behind the decision, which is the point of not using a sheet: the client can still see the thirty dots they are agreeing to write. Every number is derived client-side from the planned posts already in memory — GET /api/plan/draft loaded them before the pill could be clicked, and a pre-approval summary endpoint would be a second source for a number the client is already holding.

Provenance

  • The three counts are approvalCounts’s own arithmetic over ivy-t’s real September: 30 posts, 15 carousels and 15 reels (month-summary.md §1), so 30 captions, 30 hooks (reels and carousels both), 15 scripts (reels only). The labels are approvalRows’s shipped strings.
  • The consequence sentence is the shipped correction, verbatim. An earlier version told clients their month was locked after approval, which was false — every post stays editable by date until its own date — and the corrected line is binding.
  • The commit button is Yes, write them and the pill is Generate: “Generate” is the system’s verb for a state transition, and by the time a client is reading three counts the honest button names what they are agreeing to.

Decisions this frame carries

  • The conversation stays live behind the scrim, and it is not reachable. A modal is modal. This is the one state in the set where the dock is present but inert, and it is deliberate: approval is the one moment the surface asks for the client’s whole attention.
  • “Not yet”, not “Cancel”. The safe way out of a money decision should sound like a decision, not like an escape.
