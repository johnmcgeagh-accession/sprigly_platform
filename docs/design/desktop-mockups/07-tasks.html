Tasks — the checklist, adapted to width

The scope note holds on both form factors: the checklist is carried forward, not redesigned. What changes is where it sits. Tasks are month-wide rather than day-scoped, so the view replaces the whole plan region — grid and day column together — rather than one of its columns.

The conversation does not move. This is the state that proves the dock is a region of the shell and not a feature of the Plan view: a client can be looking at their checklist and still say “move the reel to Friday” without navigating anywhere.

A completed task ticks and moves to a Completed section (round 6, P8) rather than vanishing. The rows are capped at a 720px measure — a step label and a post title do not get better at 830px, and a checklist that runs the full width of a 1440 screen reads as a spreadsheet.

A — Earl of East · October · today is 1 October · 1440 × 900

Four groups: Overdue, Due today, This week, and Completed. The overdue rows say Late rather than a date, which is the shipped treatment — and it is the one place danger appears on a client screen for something that is not a delete. That tension is recorded in the spec rather than resolved unilaterally here.

Provenance

  • The step labels and their lead days are the real step_templates rows (0067_step_templates): reel — Script & hook (4), Shoot (3), Edit (2), Caption (1); carousel — Source shots (3), Design frames (2), Caption (1); single — Source image (2), Caption (1).
  • The due dates are those lead days applied to Earl of East’s real post dates, with today at 1 October. The arithmetic is the product’s own.
  • Which steps are ticked is recorded nowhere and is shown as a plausible early-October state — the same labelled reconstruction the phone set uses for its checklist frames. The counts in the rail and the group headings agree with it.
  • The two carousel rows name their post as Home & Space — Carousel, the assembler’s deterministic fallback form, because that post’s title is elided in the report rather than recorded.

Decisions this frame carries

  • The month title row becomes the view’s name. No month arrows, no Today, no Generate — none of them acts on a checklist, and a control that does nothing on the view you are in is worse than an absent one.
  • The rail’s count is the late count, matching the phone’s badge, so the number that made you click is the number at the top of the list.
