Detail panel — a committed post, beside the conversation

The one question this brief asked to be resolved deliberately: the detail panel and the conversation both want the right edge. They do not share it. The detail panel is a drill-down of the day, so it takes the day column’s slot; the conversation keeps its dock. Nothing reflows when a post opens.

Why not tabs, stacking, or detail-over-conversation. Tabbing them would mean opening a post closes the conversation, which contradicts the whole of exception 1 — a thing that disappears when you use the surface is not persistent. Stacking halves the thread to about 300px, which is less than one interpretation turn with three lines and its Apply row, and it moves the composer every time a post opens. Detail-over-conversation hides the thread at exactly the moment a client is most likely to say “change this one”. The deciding case is mockup 5: the reshape moment requires the month, the turn and the change to be visible together, and only this arrangement can draw it.

What it costs, stated rather than discovered later. The other posts on the day are not visible while one is open — which is exactly what happens on the phone, where a sheet covers the day. The header row above the title is the way back, and it names the day rather than saying “Back”.

Decisions this frame carries

  • Copy is a labelled button, not a bare glyph. The phone uses a 44px icon because it has no room for a word; 320px of desktop panel does, and copying is the client’s real end action — it is the one control on this surface that earns its label.
  • The action row keeps its three buttons at 44px. Same structure, same labels, same danger tint on Delete. Desktop has room to make them bigger and should not: they are not the reason anyone opened the panel.
  • The back control names the day. “Back” tells you a direction; “Thursday 1 October” tells you where you land, and it is the same string the day header carries.
