The reshape moment — say a sentence, watch the month change

This is the demo, and it is the frame that pays for the whole layout. On the phone the client speaks into a sheet that covers the month; the change lands, the sheet closes, and they go looking for what moved. Here the sentence, the interpretation and the month are on one screen, and the second frame is what happens 400 milliseconds after Apply.

Nothing about the model changes. The lines are built here from the resolved fields on each item — the verb from the action, the title from the post, the dates formatted by this surface from ISO strings. It is the rationale-evidence rule applied to consent: the client agrees to the change, not to a claim about the change.

One thing on this screen is new, and it has no API. The two ringed cells are a client-side read of the open turn’s own items[].date — the same object the lines are built from, nothing fetched and nothing written. It is listed in the spec’s wiring table as UI-only, and it is the single strongest argument for a desktop surface: the phone can show you what you agreed to, and only this can show you where it lands.

A — the turn is open: two changes, itemised, and the two days they touch ringed in the month · 1440 × 900

She has said one sentence. The agent has resolved it into two changes and has applied neither — the month beside it is exactly as it was, and the post is still on the 22nd. What the grid adds is the one thing a phone cannot: the days the turn names are ringed, so the consequence of the sentence is visible in the same glance as the sentence. Each line can be left out on its own with the ×; Discard drops the lot.

Provenance

  • The post is Earl of East’s 22 October single — date, format, pillar and caption length from docs/reports/build-d-approval-phase2.md §1. The caption text itself is not recorded, so the card states its length.
  • The turn’s structure, its status lifecycle, the per-item ×, the “Apply these 2 changes” label and the settled sentence are the shipped InterpretationTurn and its acceptance transcript (docs/reports/conversation-sheet.md).
  • The client’s sentence is illustrative and labelled as such — no real Earl of East reshape of this month is recorded. Everything it resolves to is real: a real post, its real date, and a real destination inside the month.
  • The excerpt line after the format change is formatChangeNote’s own output for a single post becoming a carousel with no hook on record.

Decisions this frame carries

  • Proposals are approved on the turn that raised them, and the rail has no Approvals item. That closes the gap the phone left open — a change raised on a phone could not be acted on there, because the approve/apply surface lived only on desktop. Now it lives in the conversation on both.
  • Apply is “Apply”, not “Approve”. Approve is what you do to somebody else’s work; this is the client’s own plan, and the button is the moment it changes. The word is fenced in a test.
  • The ringed days are non-text accent — a 2px accent-600 inset with the tint behind it, which is exactly what accent-600 is for. No text sits on them, and the meaning is also carried by the lines in the turn, so the ring is never the only channel.
  • The what-changed summary chip does not appear here. It belongs to the draft surface, where a single sentence can rewrite fourteen things at once. On a committed month the change is two lines the client just read, and a chip repeating them would be a third account of the same event.
