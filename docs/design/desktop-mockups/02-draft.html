Draft month — the provisional skin and the month summary

ivy-t, September 2026, thirty planned posts, live on UAT. The same shell as mockup 1 wearing the provisional skin: a Draft badge on the month title, dashed card edges with no shadow, and the Generate pill that mockup 1 does not have.

The month summary moves under the grid. On the phone it heads the day, because a phone shows one thing at a time and the day is that thing. Here the grid is the month, so the panel that explains the month sits with it — and it is the reason the month column has something to say once the calendar has run out of rows. Nothing in its derivation changes: the same monthSummary, the same counts, the same sections built only where there is evidence.

One copy change, and it is a real one. The closed panel says “See why these posts are here” where the phone says “Tap to see …”. There is no tap on this form factor, and a verb the client cannot perform is the kind of small dishonesty this surface has spent five rounds removing.

Provenance

  • Every line of the expanded panel — docs/reports/month-summary.md §1, the rendered output of monthSummary over ivy-t's live September rows on UAT. Counts, not percentages, because five of thirty is something a client can check by counting.
  • The two prompt rows and their order — docs/reports/summary-cta.md §4 and §5: statements first, then the ranked answerable assumption, then a divider, then the open invitation.
  • The experiment beat — position 1 in beat-grounding-build.md §2 (plan_inputs 043e6bd6, lifecycle candidate) with the title as beat-detail.md §3 renders it.
  • The card's one-line reason is a labelled reconstruction: rationaleFor's compressed sentence for this beat is not recorded anywhere, so the card shows the same fact in the form gap 4 shipped — “From what you told us: ‘…’”, quoting her own sentence.
  • 7:00 is the PostingTimes contract's documented morning example, not a stored ivy-t value. The two series times that are hers — WSG 6pm, Sunday Style 8pm — appear in mockups 4 and 5.

Decisions this frame carries

  • The Draft badge sits on the month title, not above the day. Provisional is a fact about the month, and on this form factor the month has a title row of its own. The phone's second line — “This is your September draft” — is not repeated, because the summary panel's opening sentence says the same thing eight pixels away and says it better.
  • “Not right? Tell us what to change” focuses the composer. On the phone it opens the conversation sheet; here the conversation is already open, so the honest equivalent is to put the caret in it. Same route, same consequence, one fewer thing appearing on screen.
  • The grid keeps its full height on a draft month. The summary is what scrolls, not the calendar — the month is the fixed thing and the argument about it is the long thing.
