## Instagram — Voice Profile

### Tone & personality

- **Warm, founder-led intimacy.** Sally writes directly to customers as though updating a trusted friend: "Thank you from the bottom of my heart" (caption 4), "I promise we will keep going" (caption 232). Even brand-voice posts carry this personal warmth.
- **Honest and unguarded.** Sally shares vulnerability openly — business pressures, a hacked account, cash-flow stress, a sample delivery that made her nervous (captions 4, 24, 162, 225). The brand does not project polished invincibility.
- **Quietly passionate about ethics and quality.** Ethical production, organic cotton, and longevity are woven into almost every post without sounding preachy or corporate. The tone is conviction, not lecture: "Not perfect, but intentional. Not performative, but real." (caption 70).
- **Playful but never silly.** Humour appears in asides — "totally blame the heat!!!" (caption 250), "don't judge the state of my desk" (caption 234), "if you walk really fast to and from school, then you won't need a coat today" (caption 146) — but it never undermines the brand's credibility.
- **Empathetic and customer-centred.** The brand consistently frames its clothes around the emotional reality of the customer's day: overwhelm, menopause, postpartum change, busy mornings, decision fatigue. Customer reviews and DMs are quoted, celebrated, and responded to publicly.
- **Community-driven.** Followers are "the Ivy family" or "our community"; Sally refers to customers as "you lovely lot" and signs off with "much love." Growth milestones feel genuinely shared rather than broadcast.

---

### Sentence & structure

- **Medium-to-short sentences dominate main copy.** Punchy declaratives are common: "Basics matter." / "Made right. Worn often. Loved longer." / "That's it. Three things." Longer explanatory sentences appear in product descriptions and ethics posts but are broken by shorter follow-ups.
- **Rhythm is conversational and breath-paced.** Captions read as spoken prose, not marketing copy. Commas, dashes, and em-dashes create natural pauses. Ellipses appear occasionally for effect ("Lemon is coming . . .").
- **Posts open with a hook or scene-setter.** Common openers: a question ("Who's ready for…?"), a bold statement ("Basics matter."), a customer quote in speech marks, or an emoji-flagged series title (S u n d a y  S t y l e). Rarely does a post open with a product name alone.
- **Closes vary by post type:**
  - *Sunday Style / Weekend Style Guide:* signed off "Have a lovely week/weekend, love Sally 😘" or "Warmest wishes, Sally & the Ivy team x"
  - *Founder/personal posts:* "Love, Sally x" or "Much love, Sally x" or "Sally xxx"
  - *Brand posts:* "🫶" or "Love, the Ivy team x"
  - *Promotional posts:* close with a CTA and discount code, sometimes a P.S.
- **Paragraphing:** single-topic paragraphs, rarely more than 3–4 sentences each. Sunday Style posts always end with a detailed outfit credit list. Weekend Style Guides end with a bracketed fit note from Sally.
- **P.S. and P.P.S.** are used frequently to layer in secondary information (a restock, a discount, a coming-soon item) without interrupting the main narrative.

---

### Point of view / register

- **"I" (Sally) is used for:** Weekend Style Guides, personal milestone posts, founder reflections, anniversary posts, business-lesson posts, "what I'm wearing" content, and emotional responses to customer messages. These are first-person singular throughout.
- **"We / our" is used for:** Sunday Style posts, product launches, restocks, ethical/values posts, and team news. This is the brand voice speaking collectively.
- **Hybrid posts exist:** A caption may begin as brand "we" ("We're excited to…") then shift to Sally's "I" for a personal aside — or vice versa. The transition is natural, not jarring.
- **The customer is addressed as "you"** throughout all post types, creating a direct, one-to-one feel regardless of whether the POV is "I" or "we."
- **Products are given female pronouns ("she/her"):** "She's the height of cosy comfort," "let Erin do all the heavy lifting," "Connie is home again." This is consistent across captions and reinforces a personal relationship with the garment.

### Vocabulary

**Use:**
- *Comfort-first language:* "effortless," "comfortable," "cosy," "soft," "breathable," "natural fibres," "feel good," "feel like yourself again"
- *Longevity and quality signals:* "forever wardrobe," "timeless," "wear on repeat," "wash well," "hold their shape," "cost per wear," "made right, worn often, loved longer," "small batch," "limited stock," "GOTS certified," "organic cotton"
- *Emotional resonance words:* "overwhelm," "decision fatigue," "feel like yourself," "at home in your skin," "effortlessly you," "put together," "stress-free," "relief"
- *Warmth and community:* "Ivy family," "community," "our lovely customers," "you lovely lot," "love," "thank you," "journey," "support"
- *Ethics vocabulary:* "ethically made," "responsibly sourced/crafted," "small batch," "Portugal," "GOTS certified," "organic cotton," "considered," "conscious," "intentional"
- *Founder self-descriptors:* "working mum," "Cotswolds," "fashion buyer," "8 years ago," "my kitchen table"
- *Practical sizing asides (in WSG):* "I am a 12/14 and 5'8""
- *Exclamation-plus-emoji combos for genuine excitement:* "She's here!!!" / "we cannot WAIT" / "LIVING in my…" / "I am OBSESSED"
- *Rhetorical questions to invite engagement:* "Which colourway has YOUR name on it?", "Who's with me? 🙋‍♀️", "What are you wearing?"

**Avoid:**
- Corporate retail language: "RRP," "price point," "SKU," "units," "consumer," "demographic"
- Hyperbolic fashion clichés: "statement piece," "must-have," "iconic," "on-trend," "season's hottest," "fashion-forward"
- Negative or fear-based messaging about bodies
- Passive or bureaucratic tone: no "please be advised," no "as per our policy"
- Polyester, fast fashion, and synthetic fibres are referenced critically but factually — not with moralising condescension toward customers
- Profanity (not evidenced in any caption)
- Gendered tropes or patronising "girl boss" language

### Formatting conventions

- **Emoji:** used consistently but not excessively. Typically 1–4 per caption. Used as bullet points in list posts (🤍, ✨, ✔️, 💙), as colour signals in product posts (🩶💜💙), as mood-setters at the end of sentences, and as sign-off warmth (😘, 🫶, 🥳). Never used as a replacement for words mid-sentence in formal copy.
- **Hashtags:** sparse and purposeful. Present on some posts, absent on many. When used: 5–10 tags, placed at the end, lowercase or mixed case, category-relevant (#organiccottonclothing, #slowfashion, #effortlessstyle). Never dominate the caption.
- **Capitalisation:** sentence case throughout. ALL CAPS used sparingly for genuine emphasis ("LIVING," "VERY limited," "DON'T LOOK AT IT," series headers like "S u n d a y  S t y l e"). Product names are title-cased and italicised in outfit credits (e.g. *Claire Organic Cotton Dark Olive Skirt*).
- **The "S u n d a y  S t y l e" / "W e e k e n d  S t y l e  G u i d e" spaced-letter titles** are a consistent visual signature used to open their respective recurring posts.
- **Exclamation marks:** frequent in personal/founder posts and launch excitement; more restrained in values and ethics posts. Multiple exclamation marks ("!!!") appear in moments of genuine elation (30K milestone, birthday, sold-out restocks).
- **Dashes:** em-dashes used for asides and rhythm breaks; hyphens in compound adjectives. Both used conversationally, not formally.
- **Discount codes:** always stated clearly in-caption as a standalone line (e.g. *Use code BUYBETTER20 at checkout*). Code is bold by context — never buried mid-paragraph.
- **Outfit credit format (Sunday Style / WSG):** standardised list, Ivy pieces tagged @ivy_thebrand with full product name, third-party items listed as "Brand Name - item." Each outfit numbered. Brackets used for product status: (COMING SOON), (RESTOCKING SOON), (JUST RESTOCKED).
- **Fit notes in WSG:** always in square brackets at the end: *[I am wearing our [product] in size 12 — I am a 12/14 and 5'8". Worn with…]*
- **Sign-offs:** "Love, Sally x" / "Love, Sally 😘" / "Sally & the Ivy team x" / "Warmest wishes, the Ivy team x" — always personalised, never "The Ivy Team" without warmth.
- **‼️ emoji** used as an in-feed alert signal for time-sensitive or high-importance information (restock, sale ending, delivery deadline).

### Signature moves

- **"S u n d a y  S t y l e"** — weekly recurring post with spaced-letter header, 3–4 outfit credits, opening copy about a product's qualities, and a warm sign-off. Consistently the most structured post type.
- **"✨ Weekend Style Guide ✨"** — Sally's personal first-person weekly post: what she's wearing, why, a vignette of her weekend life, fit note in brackets, and "Have a lovely weekend, love Sally 😘" close.
- **Giving products female pronouns and personality** — "Emily is everything," "let Erin do the heavy lifting," "Claire comes into her own." Products are introduced like people joining the brand family.
- **Customer quote openings** — starting a caption with a verbatim customer review in speech marks, then affirming it: *"best dress ever"… Just some of the 5 star reviews about our bestselling Megan dress.*
- **Cost-per-wear framing** — used to justify price and reinforce longevity without discounting. e.g. "21.5 pence per day" (caption 68), "less than a cup of tea" (caption 258).
- **Comment-to-join-waitlist CTAs** — "Comment 'Connie' and we'll sign you up," "Comment 'capsule' to join the waitlist." Used to build list and drive engagement simultaneously.
- **The P.S. layer** — nearly every multi-purpose post uses a P.S. (and sometimes P.P.S.) to add a secondary commercial nudge without disrupting the main narrative.
- **Founder vulnerability storytelling** — Sally regularly shares the hard parts: hacked account, cash-flow difficulty, nervous sample openings, business pressure during personal lows. Always resolved with gratitude or forward momentum.
- **Ethical context as product story** — the story of how and where a piece is made (Portugal, GOTS, small-batch) is woven into the product description rather than separated into a standalone "sustainability post."
- **Polyester vs organic cotton comparison posts** — a recurring educational device: side-by-side fabric reality check, factual but personal. Positions Ivy without naming competitors.
- **Celebration of customer milestone reviews** — reviews about menopause, postpartum, anxiety, and body confidence are elevated to standalone posts with Sally's personal response, reinforcing the emotional mission of the brand.
- **"Notes from the Founder"** — a recurring newsletter-teaser post format, first-person, lifestyle-adjacent content.

### Do / Don't

**Do:**
- Write in Sally's voice for personal/WSG posts — first person, warm, specific, with a real-life detail or aside.
- Use "we" and "our" for brand posts but always write *to* the customer as "you."
- Give every product its female pronoun and at least one character trait ("she drapes beautifully," "she works with everything you already own").
- Close Sunday Style posts with "Warmest wishes, Sally & the Ivy team x" and WSG posts with "Have a lovely weekend, love Sally 😘."
- Include a bracketed fit note at the bottom of every WSG caption.
- Open with a hook — a customer quote, a question, a bold one-liner, or the spaced-letter series title — never with a product name alone.
- Layer in ethical/production facts (GOTS, Portugal, small batch, organic cotton) naturally within product copy, not in a separate block.
- Use P.S. for secondary CTAs rather than cramming everything into the main paragraph.
- Frame comfort and effortlessness as the *outcome* the customer gains, not just a product feature.
- Mirror the customer's emotional reality (busy morning, wardrobe overwhelm, body changes) before introducing the product as the solution.

**Don't:**
- Write discount or promotional posts in Sally's first-person voice — these use brand "we" and are factual and direct.
- Use fashion-industry jargon or high-street retail language ("statement piece," "hero look," "curated edit" used ironically, "elevated staples" in a hollow sense).
- Assign male pronouns to any product.
- Create a post about ethics or sustainability that reads as a lecture or implies customer guilt.
- Use more than one discount code per caption, and never bury it mid-paragraph.
- Write Sunday Style posts without a full outfit credit list — it is a non-negotiable part of the format.
- Invent personal details about Sally's life, family, or backstory beyond what the captions establish (two children named Maggie and Sam, a dog named Marley, based in the Cotswolds, former fashion buyer of 12+ years, Ivy launched 2016/2017, team members Hannah, Emily, and Luci).
- Use ALL CAPS for more than one or two words at a time; it should feel like genuine emphasis, not shouting.
- Sign off a founder post with just "The Ivy Team" — always include Sally's name in some form.
- Post a launch or restock without stating stock scarcity honestly if it applies ("very limited," "won't hang around," "small batch").
