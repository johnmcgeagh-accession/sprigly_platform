## Instagram — Voice Profile

---

### Tone & personality

- **Warm and personally invested.** The founder (Sally) writes as someone who genuinely cares about both the product and the customer. Evidence: *"You're the ones that make this possible and you're always the ones that get me through the highs and the lows"* (caption 15); *"I promise we will keep going"* (caption 11).
- **Honest and unguarded.** Sally shares business pressures, parenting juggle, and even a dusty desk without polish. Evidence: *"The weight of running this business right now is heavy"* (caption 15); *"Please don't judge the state of my desk"* (caption 4).
- **Purposeful and quietly confident — not salesy.** Claims about quality are backed with reasoning (fibre science, cost-per-wear maths, ethical production) rather than superlatives. Evidence: caption 48 (polyester vs organic cotton breakdown); caption 35 (*"Less than a cup of tea"*).
- **Relatable and life-aware.** Captions acknowledge the reality of busy mornings, kids, working parenthood, and the mental load. Evidence: *"you've already mentally run through your day, what the kids need for school…"* (caption 18); *"it's a mix of joy and juggle"* (caption 15).
- **Celebratory of community.** Customer feedback is quoted and credited; the community's choices (colours, products) actively shape the brand. Evidence: captions 10, 50, 5.
- **Gentle humour, self-deprecating.** Used sparingly and never forced. Evidence: *"Friday (not so) Live 😂 Blame the heat!!!"* (caption 39); *"(and, yes, that's dust floating around in the video"* (caption 11).

---

### Sentence & structure

- **Mix of short punchy sentences and longer flowing ones.** Short sentences open or close a thought; longer sentences carry explanation or storytelling. Evidence: *"Getting dressed shouldn't be the hard part of your day."* (caption 18) followed by a two-line scene-setting sentence.
- **Medium post length is the norm.** Most captions are 3–6 short paragraphs. Very long posts (Sunday Style, Friday Live) are the exception and structured with clear sections (outfit lists, numbered points).
- **Opening hooks are direct or scene-setting.** Posts open with a situation the reader recognises (*"You know that feeling when you open your wardrobe and nothing feels right?"*), a bold statement (*"Fussy 'extras', stitched in layers…"*), or a named content format (✨ Weekend Style Guide ✨, S u n d a y S t y l e).
- **Closes with warmth or a soft CTA.** Many captions end with a personal sign-off (*"Have a happy weekend lovely xx"*, *"love Sally and the Ivy team x"*), a rhetorical question to invite comment, or a simple link/action prompt.
- **Bullet / list format used sparingly** — care instructions (caption 17), numbered sale reminders (captions 39, 44). Otherwise prose.
- **Sunday Style posts follow a consistent template:** spaced title + lead copy about the featured garment + three outfit lists with exact brand attributions.

---

### Point of view / register

- **Founder "I" is used in personal posts, product storytelling, and Weekend Style Guide.** Evidence: *"I'm wearing this dress on repeat this summer"* (caption 29); *"I want to make dressing as easy as possible for you"* (caption 47); *"I've personally analysed 5 different baseball style tees"* (caption 13). Sally identifies herself explicitly in several posts (captions 7, 27, 39, 44, 42).
- **Brand "we/our" is used for announcements, product descriptions, brand values, and Sunday Style.** Evidence: *"We focus on exceptional quality organic cotton fabrics"* (caption 3); *"We're here to tell you that getting dressed doesn't need to be any more complicated than this"* (caption 16).
- **The two registers blend naturally within a single post.** A caption may open with "we" then shift to "I" mid-post, or vice versa, without feeling inconsistent — this mirrors the small-team, founder-led reality.
- **Second-person "you/your" is used throughout** — the reader is always directly addressed, reinforcing the personal, conversational feel.
- **"She/her" is used as third-person for garments once introduced by name.** Evidence: *"she's designed to flatter your shape"* (caption 9); *"Pair her with wide-leg trousers"* (caption 32); *"Her cost per wear keeps going down"* (caption 35). This is a consistent brand device.

---

### Vocabulary

**Use:**
- Organic cotton descriptors: *soft, breathable, ultra-soft, midweight, GOTS-certified, 100% organic cotton*
- Ease/effortlessness: *effortless, throw on, reach for, on repeat, on autopilot, fuss-free, without thinking*
- Wearability language: *versatile, timeless, understated, wardrobe staple(s), go-to, everyday, hard-working*
- Fit/feel: *flattering, skims rather than clings/swamps, semi-fitted, relaxed, comfortable*
- Values: *ethically, responsibly, small batch, independent, quality, exceptional*
- Warm address terms: *lovelies, lovely, women* (not "ladies" or "girls")
- Named garments used as proper nouns with personality: *Maggie, Connie, Claire, Elle, Nicola, Anna, Willow*
- Phrases signalling community: *as requested by you, you asked for, your responses, the ones that make this possible*
- Cost-per-wear / longevity framing: *pence per wear, fortieth wear, three years, holds its shape wash after wash*
- *Feel good* (recurs as brand phrase: "Feel Good Summer Sale", "feel good t-shirts")

**Avoid:**
- Fashion/trend language: no *on-trend, must-have, season's hottest, fashion-forward* — captions actively argue against trend-driven buying
- Hyperbolic superlatives used vacuously (e.g., *amazing, stunning, gorgeous* about products — these appear only quoting customers)
- Corporate or formal register: no *we are pleased to announce, consumers, shoppers*
- Pushy sales language: no *buy now before it's gone* urgency-panic; urgency is stated calmly with rationale (small batch, limited stock)
- Excessive slang or Gen-Z register
- Passive voice in product descriptions

### Formatting conventions

- **Emoji:** Used consistently but not excessively — typically 2–6 per post. Emojis reinforce emotion (🫶 🥹 😘 🤍) or highlight product/colour (💙🩶💜🫒). Heart emojis (🤍🫶) are the most recurring. Emojis appear mid-sentence and at the end of lines; rarely at the start of a sentence within body copy.
- **Hashtags:** Not present in any of the 50 captions provided. The brand does not appear to use hashtags in caption copy (may be in comments — captions are silent on this).
- **Capitalisation:** Standard sentence case throughout. ALL CAPS used sparingly for genuine emphasis only: *"NOW LIVE," "FREE DELIVERY," "THE most versatile"*. Section headers within posts (e.g., "Outfit 1") are title case.
- **Exclamation marks:** Used regularly but not saturatingly — typically 1–3 per post, often at the end of a sentence that carries energy or celebration. Overuse is not evidenced.
- **Dashes:** En-dash/em-dash used conversationally to add a beat or aside: *"Soft and comfy enough to throw on and forget about for the entire day - just swapping a few things"*. Hyphen-dashes (not typographic em-dashes) are the observed style.
- **Sunday Style title format:** Spaced lettering — *S u n d a y  S t y l e* — is a consistent visual signature.
- **Weekend Style Guide title:** Flanked by ✨ emoji — *✨ Weekend Style Guide ✨* — used consistently for that content type.
- **Sign-offs:** Personal posts and weekend posts close with variants of *"Have a happy weekend lovely xx 😘"* or *"love Sally and the Ivy team x"*. Brand/announcement posts typically do not have a personal sign-off.
- **Size/wearing notes:** Appended in square brackets at the end of relevant posts: *[ Wearing Elle navy stripe dress in size 12R and Anna ecru vest in size 12 ]*.
- **Waitlist CTAs:** Formatted as a comment prompt in plain text: *Comment 'Nicola' to join the waitlist*.

---

### Signature moves

- **Garment personification.** Every named garment is given a personality and referred to as "she/her" once introduced. Descriptions frame garments as characters (*"Claire is where comfort meets style"*; *"Connie is one of those pieces that earns its place in your wardrobe by being genuinely, quietly brilliant"*).
- **Founder origin story, used repeatedly and sincerely.** The story of becoming a mum, needing better basics, and building the brand is returned to in multiple posts (captions 14, 42) without feeling repetitive because each version anchors a specific product.
- **Customer quote drops.** Verbatim customer feedback, often in inverted commas, is used mid-caption to validate a claim without Sally making it herself. Evidence: *"I couldn't live without this dress"* (caption 21); *"the most comfortable skirt I've ever worn"* (caption 9); *"the perfect sweatshirt"* (caption 45).
- **'You know that feeling…' scene-setting opener.** Posts frequently open by placing the reader inside a relatable situation before introducing the product as the solution. Evidence: captions 32, 18, 37.
- **Cost-per-wear / longevity argument.** Used as a recurring value-justification device, framing price as investment. Evidence: captions 35, 48.
- **Comment-to-join waitlist CTA.** Consistent mechanic: *Comment '[product name]' and we'll sign you up*. Used across multiple launches (Nicola, Connie, Emma).
- **Quality by subtraction.** Brand value is frequently expressed by what the clothes do *not* have: no logos, no contrast stitching, no unnecessary detail. Evidence: caption 3, caption 41.
- **Monthly round-up / "in case you missed it" format.** Recap posts summarise recent launches and tease what's next (captions 31, 49).
- **Notes from the Founder.** Named content series introduced in caption 7 — lifestyle/inspiration roundup from Sally personally.
- **Sunday Style template.** Recurring weekly content format: named products in spaced-letter title, editorial lead paragraph, three outfit lists with full styling credits including non-Ivy brands.

### Do / Don't

**DO**
- Write from Sally's point of view when the post is personal, a styling guide, or product origin story; use "we/our" for brand announcements and values posts. Blend naturally where appropriate.
- Name every garment and introduce it with a brief character description before listing its features.
- Refer to garments as "she/her" once named — this is a non-negotiable brand device.
- Open with a relatable scenario or a direct, confident statement — earn the product mention.
- Back quality claims with a specific reason (fibre science, production method, cost-per-wear) rather than adjectives alone.
- Quote real customer feedback verbatim when available — it does more than brand copy.
- Close personal posts with a warm, human sign-off (*"Have a happy weekend lovely xx 😘"*); match the energy of the post.
- Use the correct spaced-letter format for recurring content titles (*S u n d a y  S t y l e*).
- Keep urgency honest and calm: state limited stock / batch sizes as fact, not panic.
- Acknowledge the reader's real life — the juggle, the mental load, the 7am wardrobe moment.

**DON'T**
- Use trend or fashion-week language (*on-trend, must-have this season, fashion-forward*).
- Write product copy that is purely feature-list — always connect a feature to how it makes the wearer *feel* or *live*.
- Use passive voice in product descriptions.
- Add hashtags to caption copy (not evidenced in any caption provided — do not introduce them without direction).
- Overload with exclamation marks — one or two per post with genuine energy, not one per sentence.
- Invent warmth — do not write gushing or performative enthusiasm. The tone is genuine, grounded, and occasionally vulnerable.
- Refer to customers as "ladies," "girls," or "consumers" — always "women" or direct address "you."
- Use ALL CAPS beyond one or two words of genuine emphasis per post.
- Write a post that is only a sale announcement — even promotional posts contain a values statement, a styling idea, or a piece of real life.
- Forget the size/wearing credit in square brackets on any post where Sally or a team member is visibly wearing the product.
