## Instagram — Voice Profile
*Last updated: 2026-06-25*
*Sources: Apify instagram-scraper, fetches 2026-06-11 and 2026-06-12; Sally's amendment pass on 20 Sprigly-drafted captions (2026-06); client-stated brand principles doc (2026-06)*
*Content analysed: 100 posts, 2026-03-11 to 2026-06-11; 20 client-amended drafts*

---

### Brand principles (client-stated)

Sally provided these as Ivy's house rules. They corroborate the patterns observed in her edits — where a principle and her actual behaviour agree, treat the rule as high-confidence.

- **Human & real.** Write like you'd talk to a friend: warm, approachable. Be honest about what we do *and don't* do. No overpromises, no fluff.
- **Bold but not cocky.** Confidence in our quality and intentions, but no hype, gimmicks or boastful claims. Let the product and the philosophy speak for themselves.
- **Simple & clear.** Straightforward language. Short sentences over long ones. Cut anything that clutters the message.
- **Aspirational but not pretentious.** Inspire through elegance and ease. Never talk down — we make life simpler, not more complicated.
- **Timeless.** Avoid slang, trends or phrases that will date. Classic, enduring language that feels as fresh in five years as it does today.

**The litmus test:** if it sounds friendly, confident and easy to understand, it's Ivy. If it feels showy, complicated or trying too hard, it's not.

### Tone & personality
Sally writes as herself — founder, customer, and friend — not as a faceless brand. The voice is warm and personal without being gushing: she's the kind of woman who genuinely cares about your wardrobe and wants to make mornings easier. Sustainability and quality are core beliefs, not marketing copy: she writes about them the way someone would talk about something they built their life around. There's quiet confidence here — Ivy knows it's better than fast fashion and doesn't need to shout about it. The emotional register sits between supportive and matter-of-fact: never preachy, never hype-y, always grounded.

A defining trait, confirmed by her edits: **she is honest about what Ivy does and doesn't do, and never leaves a claim unexplained.** When a draft asserted something ("we use no logos"), she added the *reason* ("we believe external branding dates garments and limits their end use"). Education gets woven into product claims rather than tacked on. No overpromising.

Four adjectives: **warm, principled, unfussy, genuine**

### Sentence & structure

- Short punchy hook (often 1 sentence) → personal or relatable context → product or values point → soft CTA
- Sentence length varies: short declaratives for emphasis, longer flowing sentences for descriptions. Default to short; cut anything that clutters.
- Generous use of white space and line breaks — rarely a wall of text
- Emojis used as paragraph openers or bullet markers (not mid-sentence decoration)
- Conversational asides are characteristic — parenthetical warmth markers dropped mid-flow: "If you're unfamiliar, Connie is…", "you know, the colour that goes with everything", "to those who are asking, yes…". Use them to soften and personalise.
- Older posts used `.` on a new line as aesthetic paragraph breaks — not used post-2025

**Series formats:**

- **S u n d a y  S t y l e** (spaced-letter heading, appears weekly). **This format has two modes — do not conflate them:**
  - *Standard week* (no launch or promo): the post exists to **reduce the overwhelm of getting dressed for the week ahead**, not to sell. It lands at 8pm Sunday as women start thinking about Monday. Keep product references light, lead with warmth and simplicity, and do **not** default to the rigid "Outfit 1 / 2 / 3 + full credit block". A clean multi-outfit template that reads as a sales push is wrong for this slot.
  - *Launch / promo week*: product-forward is fine. Build 2–3 outfit combinations around one hero piece, each closing with a full product credit list (brand, product name, partner brands tagged).
- **✨ Weekend Style Guide ✨** — **one outfit only.** This is a single hero piece written as a short, personal, first-person reflection (often seasonal), not a multi-look guide and not a credit-list post. Do not generate three looks for this format. Flagged twice by the client as the most common structural error.

- Educational posts follow: problem → Ivy solution → values close
- Product launch posts follow: waitlist tease → sensory/fit description → comment CTA
- Outfits credited with full product names and partnered brand tags (Sunday Style, launch-week only)

### Vocabulary

**Use:**
- `effortless / effortlessly` (one of the most frequent words in the corpus)
- `organic cotton` (always stated fully — never just "cotton")
- `GOTS certified` / `GOTS-certified organic cotton` (used confidently, not explained every time)
- `authentic`, `ethical` (qualifiers Sally adds: "authentic organic cotton", "ethical, independent brands")
- `timeless`, `staples`, `wardrobe staples`, `building blocks of your wardrobe`
- `reach for on repeat` / `reach for again and again` / `reach for on autopilot`
- `comfortable / comfort` + `flattering` (almost always paired)
- `worn often, loved longer` / `made right, worn often, loved longer` (brand tagline — use sparingly)
- `natural fibres` (as opposed to synthetics)
- `overwhelm`, `decision fatigue` (when talking about getting dressed)
- `small batch`, `limited stock`
- `independent` (to describe Ivy as a brand)
- `intentional / with intention`
- `unapologetically`
- `simple / simply` (frequent — simplicity is a brand value)
- `let your style do the talking` (not "let *you* do the talking")
- Always state the colourway on first mention: "Emma **in dark olive** arrived", not "Emma arrived"
- Product names are women's names: Maggie, Hannah, Elle, Mabel, Claire, Dotty, Sonny, Connie, Marley, Willow, Anna, Lily, Sophie, Nancy, Nicola, Iris — always capitalised

**Avoid:**
- Hard sell language: "buy now", "limited time offer". ("Don't miss out" is normally off-limits **but acceptable in a restock context with a backward reference** — Sally kept "Don't miss out *again*" on a returning piece.)
- Hype, gimmicks, boastful claims, vague superlatives: "exceptional quality", "elevated experience"
- Trend-chasing vocabulary: "on trend", "must-have of the season"
- Luxury brand posturing: "elevated", "luxe" (occasional "luxe" appears but it's rare)
- Slang or phrasing that will date — copy should feel as fresh in five years as today
- Filler intensifiers: Sally cut "That's *genuinely* it" → "That's it". Strip "genuinely", "really", "truly" where they pad.
- Fast fashion framing: `polyester`, `fast fashion`, `high street` are referenced as foils, not as aspirations
- Generic sustainability buzzwords without grounding: "eco-friendly", "green", "planet-friendly"
- Detail language about fussy embellishment: "logo", "graphic", "contrast stitching" — these are what Ivy rejects

**Jargon:** "No jargon" in the brand principles means **no corporate or marketing jargon, and never talking down** — it does *not* mean stripping out fabric and production terms. Sally uses those confidently and her audience wants them: `midweight jersey`, `GOTS certified`, `organic cotton jersey`, `dimensional stability`, `spirality`, `pilling`. Keep them. **Technical accuracy matters:** jersey is *knitted*, not woven — air moves through the **knit**, not the "weave" (a correction Sally made directly).

### Formatting conventions

**Emojis:** Frequent but purposeful, used as visual bullet points and sentence accents, never to replace words. The 🤍 white heart is the most characteristic single emoji. Other regulars: 🫶 😘 😊 🙌 ✨ 💫 🥳 👋. Colour emojis (🤎 💙 🩶 💚) used to represent product colourways.

**Capitalisation for emphasis:** GOTS, ALWAYS, LAST, HOT (sometimes styled H-O-T) are capitalised mid-sentence for emphasis. Used sparingly so it lands.

**Exclamation marks:** Present but controlled. More common when Sally is reacting personally ("That was fun!!", "I'm off!!!"). Brand-voice posts tend toward periods and the occasional `?`. Not every sentence ends in `!`.

**Hashtags:** Largely absent in 2025–2026 posts. When used, 4–6 tags placed at the very end, focused on product type and style (`#veststyle`, `#organiccottonclothing`, `#summervests`). No branded hashtag consistent across posts. **Default: no hashtags** unless specifically promoting a product launch.

**Outfit credits:** Formatted as `Item - Brand` (spaced hyphen, not em dash), separated by `|` or line breaks. Credits appear in square brackets at the end of a post: `[ I am wearing our organic cotton Mabel in size 12 - I am a 12/14 and 5'8" ]`. Size and height always noted.

**Em dashes:** Sally almost never uses em dashes (—) in running prose. Across 100+ posts, prose em dash usage is near zero, and her amendment pass confirmed it — every draft em dash she touched was replaced with a spaced hyphen or a full stop. Do not use them in captions. Use commas, short sentences, or line breaks to create rhythm instead. The only hyphen pattern in captions is the outfit credit format (`Item - Brand`), which uses spaced hyphens.

**Timing language:** Sally hedges near-future commitments. "Coming back this month" → "coming back **later** this month" (changed three separate times). Don't pin a tighter date than the brand has committed to.

**Closing sign-offs:** The majority of posts do NOT use a personal sign-off. They close on the content itself: a CTA, outfit credits, a question, a brand statement, or an emoji. Sign-offs are reserved for specific post contexts only.

| Sign-off | When to use |
|----------|-------------|
| `love, Sally 😘` / `Have a lovely weekend, love, Sally 😘` | End-of-week Reels where Sally is the face of the content |
| `Love, Sally x` | Personal founder posts with emotional weight: welcome posts, brand philosophy statements, Sally's holiday messages |
| `Sally x` | Short casual personal Reels (packing, personal lifestyle moments) |
| `Much love, Sally x` / `Much love always, Sally x` | Community gratitude, brand milestones, emotionally significant posts |
| `Love, Sally and the Ivy team x` | Brand-level product or service posts where the message comes from the whole team |
| `Have a lovely week, love Sally & the Ivy team x` | Weekly brand-voice educational carousels from the team |
| `Happy weekend 😘` / `Have an amazing week 😘` | Casual product Reels with a brief send-off — no Love/Sally needed |
| *(no sign-off)* | Educational posts, restock announcements, waitlist CTAs, product launch posts, outfit-credits posts, UGC reposts, and most brand statements. The default for the majority of posts. |

**The most common error when replicating Sally's voice:** treating "Love, Sally x" as the default closer. It isn't. Most posts end without any sign-off at all. Overusing it strips the warmth from the posts where it actually matters.

### CTA style

Soft and community-first. Never a hard sell. **Always carry warmth — a CTA should never land flat.** Sally consistently warms ours up and adds "our": "Come and tell us" → "We'd love to know"; "DMs are always open" → "**our** DMs are always open and we love hearing from you"; "Questions about our fabrics?" → "just pop us a DM". The dominant CTAs are:

- **Comment CTAs for waitlists:** `Comment 'Nicola' to join the waitlist` — this is the current primary growth mechanic
- **DM invitation:** `our DMs are always open and we love hearing from you` / `just pop us a DM`
- **Link in bio:** used but not overused — `head to the link in our bio`
- **Sizing/fit invite:** `get in touch on email or by DM if you have any sizing or fit questions` — appears frequently, positions Ivy as a personal stylist
- **Follow prompt:** used on intro/brand posts, not product posts
- **Engagement invite:** `Let us know how you've been wearing yours 👇` / `Have we missed anything?`

Posts rarely close on a pure product push. The ask is almost always softened with community language or an offer to help.

### Replication notes

**Sally's voice vs. brand voice:** Some posts are written in Sally's first person ("I'm wearing…", "Can I tell you a story?") and others in a softer brand "we". When writing for product posts or Sunday Style, use "we/our". When writing founder stories, discovery posts, or personal outfit posts (including Weekend Style Guide), use "I" — Sally's voice. Don't mix them in a single post.

**Register by post type (authoritative).** Register is decided by the *type* of post, not by who happens to publish it. Use this map:

| Post type | Register |
|-----------|----------|
| Weekend Style Guide (WSG) | **I** (Sally's first person — single hero, personal reflection) |
| Sunday Style | **we** (brand) |
| Founder notes / origin / "Notes from the Founder" | **I** |
| Ours vs Theirs (founder-to-camera comparison) | **I** |
| Testimonial / "What our customers see" | **we** (brand curating customer love) |
| Educational / care guides | **we** |
| Ethical / factory / sustainability | **we** (informational, not founder-personal) |
| Product launches & offers, Sunday Style, styling, outfit, brand | **we** |

**WSG ≠ Sunday Style — do not conflate them.** They are adjacent weekend formats with *opposite* registers: **Sunday Style = we**, **WSG = I**. Never apply WSG's first-person "I reach for…" lines to a Sunday Style post or vice versa. (They are now separate categories so this can't be lumped.)

**Register beats whoPosts.** A post tagged "Sally posting" is not automatically first-person — e.g. a launch Sally publishes is still brand "we". Only the genuinely founder-voice types above (founder notes, WSG, Ours-vs-Theirs) are "I".

**Garment personification — lean into the affection.** Garments are referred to with female pronouns (`her`, `she`), and Sally treats them with genuine warmth, not just grammatical convention: "Hello, grey marl Connie, how we've missed you 🙌", "Connie came back with a new violet friend". Push past flat she/her into real fondness.

**Never leave a claim unexplained.** Every product assertion should carry its reason. "We don't use logos" gets "we believe external branding dates garments and limits their end use". Pilling gets tied to polyester content. This is what keeps the copy honest rather than salesy.

**Ground abstractions in concrete, relatable anchors.** "20 pence a day" → "(about the same as 1 banana)". A real-world comparison beats a bare figure.

**Specifics over vague generality.** "A few more pieces are heading our way" → "a new edit will drop". Monthly recaps name the actual launches ("Maggie in 5 new colours, Connie came back with a violet friend, Emma has found her feet"). Don't write round-ups in placeholders.

**Product naming convention matters:** Always use the garment's proper name and full fabric description on first mention: `Hannah Midweight Organic Cotton Violet/Ecru Multi Stripe T-Shirt`. In body copy, refer to the garment by first name only after introduction. Always state the colourway on first mention.

**Sustainability is embedded, not announced:** Don't lead with "eco" or "sustainable". Weave in `organic cotton`, `GOTS certified`, `natural fibres`, and `made right` organically. Ivy's ethics are table stakes, not a campaign.

**Timeless copy, like timeless garments.** The same logic Ivy applies to its clothes applies to its writing: avoid anything that will date. If a phrase wouldn't read well in five years, cut it.

**Confidence without exclusivity:** Ivy speaks to every woman who's struggled to find clothes that fit and last — not a narrow aesthetic. Avoid language that makes the customer feel inadequate or trend-behind. Never talk down.

**The Ivy litmus test (apply to every draft):** friendly, confident and easy to understand → it's Ivy. Showy, complicated or trying too hard → it's not.

**Writing against AI patterns:** The most common ways AI writing slips into Ivy captions:
- Em dashes used for rhythm or drama (replace with commas or short sentences)
- Vague superlatives with no substance: "exceptional quality", "elevated experience", "vibrant community"
- The rule of three as a rhetorical wrapper: "clarity, comfort, and confidence"
- Present-participle tail phrases that pad sentences: "...ensuring every woman feels her best"
- Corporate warmth language: "commitment to", "dedicated to delivering", "passionate about"
- Filler intensifiers: "genuinely", "really", "truly" where they add nothing
- Generic positive closers: "exciting times ahead", "we can't wait to share more"
- Sign-offs added to posts that don't call for one (see sign-off table above)
- Flat CTAs with no community warmth, and CTAs missing the "our" before "DMs"

Replace all of the above with short declarative sentences, specific product details, and Sally's actual voice: first person, a bit of genuine excitement, and no padding.
