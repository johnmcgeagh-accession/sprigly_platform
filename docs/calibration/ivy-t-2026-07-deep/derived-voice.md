## Instagram — Voice Profile

---

### Tone & personality

- **Warm and founder-led.** Sally writes as herself throughout, sharing personal anecdotes, emotions, and daily life. The brand voice feels human because it *is* a human. ("I started Ivy as a first time mum in 2016, when Maggie was still a baby.")
- **Quietly passionate, never preachy.** Ethical and sustainable credentials are stated plainly as facts or "non-negotiables," not as moral lectures. The tone trusts the audience to care. ("We only use certified organic cotton. Not sometimes. Not 'where possible.' Always.")
- **Genuinely empathetic.** Sally positions herself as someone who *had the same problem* her customer has. Customer reviews and messages are shared with visible emotion. ("Some messages stay with you.")
- **Celebratory but grounded.** Milestones (30K, birthday, 8 years) are met with heartfelt gratitude, not hype. ("We absolutely wouldn't be here without your support.")
- **Self-deprecating and real.** Behind-the-scenes moments include chaos, messy desks, delayed stock, Instagram refusing to go live. She doesn't airbrush the small-business reality. ("Don't judge the state of my desk.")
- **Decisive and opinionated about quality.** On polyester, fast fashion, and detail-for-the-sake-of-it, the voice becomes more pointed — clear convictions without aggression.

---

### Sentence & structure

- **Sentence length varies deliberately.** Short punchy statements ("Basics matter.") alternate with longer flowing passages that build empathy or context. Three-word fragments are used for emphasis.
- **Paragraph breaks are frequent and intentional.** Long captions are broken into short blocks of two to four lines. A single full stop on its own line (". ") is used as a visual pause in earlier captions, though this fades in more recent posts.
- **Posts typically open with a hook** — a direct address ("Oh, hi!"), a bold claim ("Basics matter."), a question, or a short emotional scene-setter ("I want to tell you a story."). They rarely open with the product name.
- **Posts typically close with one of:** a warm sign-off from Sally with a kiss ("Love, Sally 😘"), a call-to-action, a reflective thought, or a tagline couplet ("Made right · Worn often · Loved longer").
- **Sizing notes appear in square brackets** at the foot of Weekend Style Guide posts: a consistent functional footer separate from the main voice. ("[ I am wearing our Connie sweatshirt in violet size 10…]")
- **Sunday Style posts follow a fixed architecture:** intro paragraph, outfit rationale, then a tabular outfit list. The header is always spaced out: "S u n d a y  S t y l e".

---

### Point of view / register

- **First-person "I" (Sally's voice)** is used for founder stories, personal styling, emotional reflections, business lessons, milestone celebrations, Weekend Style Guide posts, and customer message responses. This is the dominant register across the account.
- **Brand "we/our"** appears in product launches, event announcements, campaign copy, ethical credentials, and Sunday Style intros written by the team. It also surfaces in replies to reviews ("This is exactly why we do what we do").
- **Switching within a single post is common** — Sally will open in "I," shift to "we" when speaking for the brand, then return to "I" to close personally. This is consistent and not jarring.
- **"You"** is used extensively to address the customer directly, creating a one-to-one conversational feel regardless of whether the post is written as "I" or "we."

---

### Vocabulary

**Use:**
- *Organic cotton, GOTS certified, organic cotton jersey, certified organic cotton* — always named in full, never shortened to just "cotton"
- *Staples, forever pieces, timeless, capsule wardrobe, forever wardrobe* — the brand's vocabulary for longevity
- *Reach for, turn to, rely on, go-to, live in* — comfort and habitual wearing
- *Effortless, fuss-free, versatile, unfussy, understated* — the desired aesthetic
- *Comfortable, comfort-first, soft, buttery soft, cloud-like, softest* — fabric feeling, used with genuine enthusiasm
- *Ethical, responsibly made, conscious, small batch, slow fashion* — production values
- *Made right · Worn often · Loved longer* — the brand tagline (used as a closer or caption standalone)
- *Women, every woman, our customers, our community, the Ivy family* — inclusive audience terms
- *Flattering, skims, body-skimming, semi-fitted* — cut language; never "slimming"
- *Cost per wear, investment, pennies per wear* — economic argument for quality
- *Washability, holds its shape, doesn't pill, doesn't shrink, dimensional stability* — specific quality promises
- *Portugal, Portuguese factory, ethically made in Portugal, GOTS certified* — provenance as a trust signal
- *Made in small batches, limited stock, won't last long* — scarcity, stated as factual not manipulative
- *Dopamine dressing, colour pop, add a pop of colour* — used sparingly for new colours
- Piece names used as proper nouns with personality: *she, her* ("She's the kind of piece you'll reach for")

**Avoid:**
- Hyperbolic marketing language: "luxury," "exclusive," "premium" as empty descriptors (the brand uses "premium" only when it follows specific fabric evidence)
- "Fast fashion" as a slur applied to competitors by name — it's used as a category, never naming brands
- Corporate jargon: "synergy," "curated collection," "key pieces," "investment piece" (they say "forever piece" instead)
- Excessive superlatives without proof ("the best brand ever")
- Gendered size language: no "flattering for your figure" in a body-critical sense
- Urgency manufactured through FOMO alone — scarcity is always tied to the real reason (small batch production)
- Exclamation marks used mechanically; they appear when genuine excitement is present, not as default punctuation

---

### Formatting conventions

- **Emoji:** Used freely and warmly, not decoratively. Common choices: 🫶 (affection/trust), 🤍 (brand values), 💙 (product colours, loyalty), ✨ (style guides/launches), 😘 (Sally's sign-off), 🥳 (celebrations), ❤️ (gratitude), 😞 (genuine disappointment), 🌱 (sustainability). Emojis open bullet-style lists in some posts. Rarely more than one or two per sentence.
- **Hashtags:** Grouped at the end, typically 6–20 tags. Mix of brand (#ivythebrand, #ivy_thebrand), product (#organiccottonclothing, #womenstshirts), value (#slowfashion, #ethicalclothing), and lifestyle (#effortlessstyle, #feelgoodclothing). Some older captions use no hashtags at all for more editorial posts.
- **Capitalisation:** Sentence case throughout the main body. ALL CAPS used sparingly for genuine emphasis ("HELL YES", "‼️ NOT A DRILL"). Product names are title-cased (Connie, Claire, Megan). Tagline words in full caps as a sign-off block ("SUSTAINABLE | ESSENTIAL | VERSATILE | ORGANIC").
- **Exclamation marks:** Present but earned. Multiple in the same post when Sally is genuinely excited about a launch or milestone. Absent from more considered, reflective posts.
- **Dashes:** Em-dashes used for asides and rhythm breaks (—). Hyphens used for compound adjectives. Ellipsis (…) used occasionally for dramatic pause or anticipation.
- **Section separators:** Pipe character (|) used in tagline sign-offs and product category lists. Bullet points (✔️, 🤍, 💫, ‼️, star emojis) used as list openers in product or ethics posts.
- **Outfit credits:** Always listed at the end of Sunday Style and Weekend Style Guide posts in a plain, consistent format: *Product Name @ivy_thebrand* then *Category - Brand*.
- **The Sunday Style header is always styled with letter-spacing:** "S u n d a y  S t y l e" — this is a visual signature.
- **Sign-offs:** Sally's personal posts close with "Love, Sally 😘" or "Much love, Sally x" or "Sally x". Team posts close with "Love, the Ivy team x" or "Warmest wishes / Sally & the Ivy team x". No sign-off on short product posts.

---

### Signature moves

- **The founding story, told in fragments.** Sally's origin story (new mum, poor t-shirts, fashion industry background, Portuguese factory) appears across multiple posts in different lengths — a short version, a medium version, a full version — allowing new followers to encounter it naturally over time.
- **Personifying products as "she/her."** Every named piece is referred to as *she* and given personality. Launched pieces are "welcomed" into the range.
- **The "cost per wear" argument.** Appears repeatedly as a concrete trust-builder. Sally works through the maths explicitly. ("21.5 pence per day.")
- **Customer message posts.** A recurring format: screenshot or paraphrase of a real customer message, Sally's emotional response, and a bridge back to the brand's mission. Always genuine, never triumphalist.
- **The Weekend Style Guide (WSG).** Sally's personal weekly post. Outfit diary format, first-person, casual, includes what she was actually doing that weekend. Closes with a sizing footer in square brackets.
- **"Comment [word] to join the waitlist."** Standard launch mechanic, always paired with the product name as the trigger word.
- **Ethical contrast device.** Comparing Ivy's approach to unnamed high-street alternatives through fabric content, supply chain, or washability — always factual, never naming competitors.
- **The "you" empathy opener.** Many posts begin by describing the customer's problem in second person before offering the solution. ("You're tired of t-shirts losing their shape…")
- **Milestone celebrations with community credit.** Every follower count, birthday, or record is framed as something the community made happen, with explicit thank-yous to long-standing customers and new followers alike.
- **Behind-the-scenes honesty.** Stock delays, cash flow challenges, Instagram failures, messy desks, sample anxiety — shared openly, which builds trust in the good news.

### Do / Don't

**DO**
- Write product descriptions that lead with *how the piece will make the wearer feel*, then follow with fabric, fit, and care details
- Use Sally's first-person voice for any post that involves personal experience, opinion, or emotion; switch to "we" for announcements and brand statements
- Give every piece a personality — use "she/her" pronouns and give the piece agency in the wardrobe
- Include honest sizing, fit notes and wear details — stock sizes, how Sally wears her own pieces, guidance on sizing up or down
- Name the fabric standard every time: "GOTS-certified organic cotton," not just "organic cotton"
- Let genuine emotion land before pivoting to a CTA; the feeling must come first
- Reference Portugal and the factory as a trust signal, not just a credential checkbox
- Acknowledge difficulty (delays, business pressures, bad days) as a way to deepen connection before resolving with warmth
- Close personal posts with a warm, named sign-off: "Love, Sally 😘" or "Much love, Sally x"
- Use customer words verbatim in quotation marks rather than paraphrasing when sharing reviews

**DON'T**
- Lead with product features before the customer's emotional context
- Use pressure-based urgency language that isn't grounded in a real reason (always tie scarcity to small batch production)
- Write descriptions of fabric as abstract marketing words without connecting them to how the fabric *performs* in real life
- Add humour as a stylistic choice if it isn't natural — the wit here comes from real-life moments, not copywriter jokes
- Use generic sustainability vocabulary ("eco-friendly," "planet-first") without a specific, evidenced claim
- Over-explain or pad — short fragments are as valid as long paragraphs; know when to stop
- Ignore new followers — the brand consistently acknowledges and welcomes them; any milestone post should do the same
- Write Sunday Style or Weekend Style Guide intros without some version of the empathy hook ("you know that feeling when…") before the outfit content
- Refer to Ivy pieces as "products" or "items" — they are always "pieces," "staples," "styles," or named directly
- Forget the founding story is a living asset — it can be retold in part or in full whenever the audience is new or the moment calls for it
